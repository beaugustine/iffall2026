Tennessee Peach Pudding

When peach season hits, look no further than this warm, comforting cobbler-style peach pudding. Get that vanilla ice cream ready!

Yield: 8 Servings

Prep: 20 mins | Cook: 40 mins | Total time: 1 hr

Ingredients

  • 1 cup all-purpose flour
  • 1/2 cup sugar
  • 2 teaspoons baking powder
  • 1/2 teaspoon salt
  • 1/2 teaspoon ground cinnamon, optional
  • 1/2 cup 2% milk
  • 3 cups sliced peeled fresh or frozen peaches

Topping:

  • 1 1/2 cups water
  • 1/2 cup sugar
  • 1/2 cup packed brown sugar
  • 1 tablespoon butter
  • 1/4 teaspoon ground nutmeg
  • Vanilla ice cream or heavy cream, optional

Directions

  1. Preheat oven to 400°. Combine flour, sugar, baking powder, salt and, if desired, cinnamon. Stir in milk just until combined; fold in peaches. Spread into a greased 8-in. square baking dish.

  2. For topping, combine water, sugars, butter and nutmeg in a large saucepan. Bring to a boil, stirring until sugars are dissolved. Pour over peach mixture. Bake until filling is bubbly and a toothpick inserted in topping comes out clean, 40-50 minutes. Serve warm or cold, with ice cream if desired.

1 serving: 253 calories, 2g fat (1g saturated fat), 5mg cholesterol, 269mg sodium, 57g carbohydrate (44g sugars, 1g fiber), 3g protein.

Recipe Websites

  • New York Times Cooking is an effective recipe website because it highlights recipes in an easily digestible format. On the homepage, users are presented with a recipe of the day, recent articles, and a simple navigation system to find recipes within different categories.

  • Pinch of Yum is a site that I often use to find recipes because they include many categories for searching recipes. When users first enter the site, they are invited to explore recipes categorized as 'Dinner,' 'Quick and Easy,' 'Soups,' and 'Kid-Friendly.' When viewing an individual recipe, the page includes writing about the recipe, but has a 'Jump to Recipe' button if users are uninterested. The page also features photos of the process of making the recipe, so users know what it should look like at various stages.

  • Epicurious is a good recipe website because it offers the user access to a large amount of recipes without being overwhelming. The pages for each recipe are nicely laid out, with a large image of the dish at the top, a brief description, and a clear list of ingredients and directions that are visible at the same time.

Non-Recipe Websites

  • The Atlantic is an interesting site to consider because it is laid out similarly to many recipe websites, but has a different purpose. I like the simple design used to foreground the articles themselves.

  • Pinterest is an image-forward site, which I think could be an effective way to communicate recipes. It is also community driven, allowing users to share their experiences publicly.

  • Whichbook is a website that helps readers choose their next book through prompts about what they're looking for. I think that this concept and layout could also be useful for choosing recipes, and I also like the simplicity of the site.
